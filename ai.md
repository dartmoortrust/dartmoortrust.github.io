# Using AI

Over the past years, we have been experimenting with using AI to support the work of cataloguing our images. When used correctly, the quality of the responses is often good enough to use. It is certainly better than having nothing and can often work as a seed of information that we can always build upon.

The key to a good response is a good prompt. This page will be updated with the latest guidance.

Google's Gemini is currently free and powerful. So the current recommendation is to use that for catalogueing where you need support.

### Instructions

1. Go to [Google Gemini](https://gemini.google.com)
2. Enter the following text (prompt) "Act as an academic researcher writing for a photo archive website. Provide a professional, two-paragraph description of the attached image, which is likely from Dartmoor. In the first paragraph, describe the physical object and its general historical function. In the second paragraph, provide context by explaining what can be inferred about its geology, location, and age based on visual evidence. Use a formal, scholarly tone and bold key terms. Do not use bullet points, headings, or lists; present the response as two cohesive blocks of text."
3. Right click on the image you are editing and choose "Copy Image"
4. Return to Gemini and right click on the prompt and choose paste. You should see a small verion of the image appear above the prompt.
5. Click the arrow image to start the query.
6. Check the response carefully. Where you think the AI could do better with some additional information you can follow this up with some clarification - for example "We know this was taken in Tavistock".

When used correctly you should manage to get a few lines/paragraphs to copy into the details box of the edit window.

> Note: this will be automated in the near future after some further testing and feedback.
